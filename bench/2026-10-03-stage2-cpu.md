# Stage 2 span models on CPU — sizing and speed (2026-10-03)

Question: can BERT-class span models run on CPU/RAM inline, before a request leaves the host?
Answer on this host: **memory yes (~1.5–1.8 GB, flat), speed no for large inputs** — 0.5–1.5 s per KB
on 3 cores. ADR-0005's "tens of ms per KB" assumption was 10–50× optimistic.

## Setup

- Host: AMD Ryzen 7 3700X (Zen 2, 8 cores, no SMT, AVX2, no AVX-512/VNNI), 64 GB RAM. Kent's
  llama-server shares CPU (`-ncmoe 40 -t 5`) and RAM (22 GB); it was idle during every run.
- Every run: `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=4G -p MemorySwapMax=0 nice -n 19 taskset -c 5-7`
  (cap verified: a 400 MB allocation under 200 MB is killed, exit 137). Swap stayed 0.
- Text: PPE synthetic corpus (seed 20261002) concatenated to 160 B / 1 KB / 10 KB / 100 KB.
  Tokens per KB: ~400 (Privacy Filter, o200k), ~516 (mDeBERTa).
- Larger inputs split into 1,500-character chunks for GLiNER2 (≈ its 512-token window); the
  Privacy Filter runtime windows internally (4,096 tokens, halo overlap).

## Results (median wall time, 3 threads unless noted)

| Detector | 160 B | 1 KB | 10 KB | 100 KB | RAM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPE Stage 1 rules (1 thread) | 0.14 ms | 0.8 ms | 9.4 ms | 201 ms | small |
| OpenAI Privacy Filter, privacy-filter.cpp q8 | 78 ms | 470 ms | 4.7 s | 92 s | 1.8 GB flat |
| same, 1 thread | 210 ms | 1.36 s | 13.6 s | 268 s | 1.8 GB |
| GLiNER2-PII, PyTorch fp32 (10 labels) | 387 ms | 1.4 s | 15.0 s | 156 s | 1.8 GB (3.0 GB at load) |
| same, 1 thread | 773 ms | 2.9 s | 31.5 s | 328 s | same |
| GLiNER2-PII, ONNX int8 (third-party export, 7 fixed labels) | 448 ms | 1.5 s | 17.5 s | 174 s | 1.5 GB |
| OpenAI Privacy Filter, HF transformers 5.18 fp32 | — | — | — | killed at 33 GB | grows with length² |

Privacy Filter throughput: ~290 tok/s per thread, ~820 tok/s on 3 threads (434 at 40k tokens),
consistent with the runtime's published 3,500 tok/s on 12 Zen 4 threads.

## Where the time and memory go

- **HF transformers Privacy Filter on CPU:** `_supports_sdpa = False` → `eager_attention_forward`
  computes the full 14 × T × T score tensor, then masks it to the ±128 band. At T = 39,879 one fp32
  tensor is 83 GiB (banded: 547 MiB). The model is fine; the runtime is not. privacy-filter.cpp
  (ggml, MIT, CI parity vs HF) and OpenAI's `opf` implement the band natively.
- **GLiNER2:** preprocessing 1 ms; the DeBERTa-v3 encoder forward is the cost (fp32: 394 ms at
  140 tokens, 1,355 ms at 512, 2,390 ms at 774; dynamic int8: 204 / 1,298 / 2,080 ms). ~24 GFLOP per
  140 tokens against ~100–170 GFLOP/s achievable on 3 AVX2 cores → within ~2× of the hardware bound.
  278M parameters, 192M of them the multilingual embedding table.
- **ONNX int8 is not faster here** (MatMulNBits q8 on AVX2 without VNNI).
- **f16 GGUF on Zen 2:** no native f16 arithmetic; stopped as a slower path.

## Stage 1 findings (fix regardless)

- 10 KB takes 9.4 ms against the < 5 ms target.
- 10 KB → 100 KB costs 21× instead of 10× (super-linear somewhere in Stage 1).
- **Fixed 2026-10-16:** two pairwise scans (overlap resolution; phone vs date/time spans) replaced by
  bisect over disjoint sorted spans; output byte-identical (37,372 findings, corpus + 400 KB doc, all
  modes). Now 0.68 / 0.70 / 0.76 ms/KB at 10 / 100 / 400 KB (was 0.80 / 1.70 / 5.1); guarded by
  `tests/detectors/test_scaling.py`. 10 KB is 6.8 ms, still over 5 ms: `build_shadow`'s per-character
  loop is ~57 % of it (an ASCII / already-NFKC fast path is the next candidate).

## Published comparisons

- privacy-filter.cpp README (Ryzen 9 7900, 12 threads, fp32): 3,564 / 3,490 / 2,332 tok/s at
  512 / 2,048 / 8,192 tokens; HF 2,171 / 978 / 304 tok/s, OOM ≈ 16k tokens on GPU.
- GLiNER2 production write-up (Rust, ONNX int8 encoder, ≤ 512 tokens): ~250 ms p50 on 4 vCPU,
  ~800 MB.
- GLiNER2 paper: 130 ms (5 labels) to 208 ms (50 labels) on CPU.

## Not measured

Accuracy on PPE's corpus (speed only here); GPU; all 8 cores (would contend with Kent); the
